Reflection

Every claim here points back to the 101, 102, 103 and Cash tabs on this page, plus audit_trail.json and resolve_tickets.json. Token counts are input + output summed from the audit trail.

1 · How we grade the band, and why

Bro, "the ticket says resolved" ain't a grade. The board stamped all three green, but a green stamp with bad cash math still loses points. So we score every ticket on five things, in order of how bad it is to get them wrong:

1. Money is rightDid cash move only on a human signature, for the amount in the database, and does the ending balance tie out to cash_accounts? This goes first because a wrong dollar is the only mistake you can't undo with a re-run.
2. Rules heldShop date 2026-08-31 for overdue, vendor lead times from vendors, no shipping on an open invoice, no price under unit_cost, no real emails. One break here is a fail, even if the outcome looks fine.
3. Facts came from toolsEvery number in the decision and the customer draft (0 tees, 8 hoodies, $840, $2,400, 9/02, 5 lead days) should trace to a tool result in the audit trail, not to a guess.
4. Lean bandDelegations and tokens against our Expected plan. Calling everyone scores low, and an extra hop costs about 5–8k tokens on Luna.
5. Good for a humanIs the call clear, and would Tauhid or the AI Club actually understand the draft? Does it avoid telling the customer about our unpaid bills?

2 · Expected vs actual, ticket by ticket

101Expected: 3 delegations (Boss→Inventory→Accounting, Boss→CS), ending at waiting_approval with $840 queued. Actual pass 1: exactly that chain and that status, plus an extra CS→Inventory check and one refused CS→Boss. What we missed in the plan: we assumed paying 501 would finish the ticket, but paying the bill doesn't put a tee on the shelf. Pass 2 came back blocked because there was no way to reorder. We added the "note the reorder and resolve" rule, and pass 3 closed it with 2 delegations. Facilities sat out every time, as planned.
102Expected: 2 delegations (Boss→Facilities→Accounting), $2,400 queued, waiting_approval. Actual: same path and amount, plus a direct Boss→Accounting call (3 in total). Inventory and CS sat out, as planned. This was the closest match to the plan of the three.
103Expected: 3 delegations (Boss→Inventory, Boss→Accounting for the margin, Boss→CS), a discount around 10–15%, 8 now and 12 after a restock. Actual: 10% at $52.20 and the 8/12 split, nailed it. But pass 1 ended blocked, not waiting_approval, because Inventory found the 501 tie to Bulldog. The plan was wrong, and the agents were right: the three tickets weren't independent, so ticket order and the human signature on 101 decided when 103 could close.

The big lesson: our plan treated each ticket as its own thing. The data had 101 and 103 sharing one vendor and one unpaid invoice, and all three sharing one $3,400 checkbook. Only by luck did both bills fit, leaving $160.

3 · What would've been simpler as one agent with tools

Honest take: all three tickets would run fine on one Luna agent holding all 13 agent-safe tools. Every fact the specialists found came from one MCP call or two. Where the team cost us:

  • 102 is the poster child. One agent does get_ticket → get_lease → get_cash_balance → request_payment → update_ticket. That's five tool calls with no hand-offs. The team spent about 22k tokens on pass 1 and another 21k on pass 2 just to confirm a paid bill that a single list_payments call would show.
  • 103 is pricing math: check_inventory + get_pricing + list_vendors, then a reply. That's about 34k and 32k tokens split across three agents for a decision that's one line of arithmetic: $58 × 0.9 = $52.20 > $22.
  • 101 is the only one where the hand-off added something real. Inventory → Accounting is the moment "out of stock" becomes "we owe the vendor money", and keeping the payment request inside Accounting's tool scope meant Inventory and CS couldn't queue a payment even if they got confused.

So why keep the band? Not for brains, for guardrails and receipts. Each agent's tool allowlist is least privilege (CS can't touch cash), the delegation guard stopped a real loop on 101, and the audit trail reads like a who-did-what. The right shape for this shop is probably one main agent with the read tools, plus a locked-down Accounting agent as the only one that can call request_payment. That keeps the safety and drops most of the token bill.

4 · Three new problems this team CAN solve with what we built

  1. Someone wants crest mugs. CC-MUG-CREST shows 0 on hand. Boss→Inventory: check_inventory says 0, and list_vendors says Elm City Gifts makes mugs, ships in 3 days, and has no open invoice, so can_ship is true. Inventory records the reorder with the earliest arrival 2026-09-03, and CS drafts a reply with the $14 list price from get_pricing. Same path as 101 pass 3, no cash, resolved in one pass.
  2. October rent. Lease 1 now shows next due 2026-10-02 (our approve tool set that). When that notice comes in, Boss→Facilities verifies $2,400 against the lease, Accounting runs request_payment, and the tool refuses, because checking is $160. The ticket goes blocked with "can't cover rent" instead of overdrawing. That's the no-negative-balance rule doing its job, which proves the guardrail with real numbers from our Cash tab.
  3. A club wants 30 blue hats with a discount. CC-HAT-BLUE has 40 on hand, cost $6, list $22. It's the 103 flow without the restock headache: Inventory confirms 40 ≥ 30, Accounting sets a discount floor above $6, and CS drafts the quote. One pass, resolved, no human signature needed because no cash moves.

5 · Three new problems this team CANNOT solve yet, and what we'd need

  1. Getting paid. The AI Club says "yes" to 20 hoodies at $52.20, which is $1,044 coming in. We have no sales table and no tool that adds cash ("cash only goes out" is baked into the app), so checking stays stuck at $160 and October rent fails. Need: a sales/orders table, record_sale(ticket_id, sku, qty, price) and record_payment_received tools that credit cash_accounts, and a Sales/Register agent to create orders and reserve stock. Customer payments should still need a human to confirm the money actually arrived.
  2. Real purchasing and follow-up. "Note it, resolve" was our workaround: the reorders for 1 tee and 12 hoodies live only in ticket notes. Nobody actually orders anything, and nobody checks on 9/05 whether they showed up. Need: a purchase_orders table, create_purchase_order (human approval, like payments, at unit_cost × qty: 12 × $22 = $264, which the $160 balance couldn't cover today), receive_stock to bump inventory, a scheduler that reopens the ticket on the arrival date, and a Purchasing agent. Sending the PO to Bulldog would also need an outbound-email tool, gated behind a human click, since we never contact real vendors.
  3. Seeing the cash crunch coming. After this run we're at $160 with $2,400 due 10/02. No agent could warn us, because every tool answers "what is" and none answers "what's next". 103 also flagged that invoice 501's line item references the tee, which hints at needing to audit what a bill is really for. Need: forecast_cash(days) over leases, open invoices, open POs and expected sales, list_invoices(due_before), a line-items table, and a Finance/CFO agent that runs weekly, ranks bills by due date and vendor impact, and tells a human "pay this first, chase that receivable", before a ticket ever goes blocked.

The thread through all of it: the agents were most useful wherever the tools gave them hard numbers (stock, lead days, the $3,400 → $160 math), and they got stuck exactly where a tool was missing. More tools beat more agents.

Across all three tickets

  • The tickets are connected. Paying invoice 501 on ticket 101 is also what unblocks the hoodie restock on 103. If 101 runs first, 103 should see that payment already pending and not queue it twice. The duplicate guard should catch it if it tries.
  • The cash fits, but only just. $840 + $2,400 = $3,240 against $3,400, which leaves $160. Both requests should queue, and Accounting should call out how thin that is.
  • Lean on purpose. We expect 8 delegations across 3 tickets, not 4 specialists × 3 tickets = 12. Facilities only touches 102.
